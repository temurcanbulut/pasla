Pass files to the computer next to you.

Pasla moves files, folders and text between the computers and phones on your home or office network. No internet, no account, no cloud: one small program, encrypted from device to device.

How it works

Three steps the first time. After that, just drag and drop.

  1. 1

    Run it on both computers

    Download the file and double-click it. The interface opens in your browser, and computers running Pasla on the same network show up within seconds.

  2. 2

    Pair them once

    Both screens show the same 6-digit code. If it matches, confirm on both. From then on the two devices recognise each other, even when their IP addresses change.

  3. 3

    Drag and drop

    Drop files or whole folders onto the device. The other side accepts and the transfer starts. Folder structure is kept, and nothing is ever overwritten.

Phones too, without an app

Press Connect a phone on the computer and scan the QR code. The same 4-digit number appears on both screens; if it matches, allow it on the computer.

Now the phone can send photos, files and text to the computer, and download what the computer sends it. It works in the browser you already have, on iPhone and Android.

  • Nothing to install on the phone
  • The phone port opens only while you need it
  • An unused phone is disconnected after 30 minutes

Small program, the things you need

Finds your devices

Computers on the same network appear by themselves. Devices on another subnet can be added by IP once.

Files, folders, text

Whole folder trees, screenshots pasted from the clipboard, or a link or note to copy on the other screen.

As fast as your network

Files stream straight to disk and are checked with SHA-256 on arrival. Several files go in parallel.

Truly offline

The interface, fonts and icons are built in. Pasla never contacts anything outside your network.

You decide what arrives

Every transfer asks first, unless you trust a device to send without asking. Program files are flagged.

Command line, if you like

pasla send -to Office D:\Photos sends through the running Pasla, for scripts and big batches.

Made to be trusted on your network

A local network is not automatically a safe one. Pasla assumes someone else may be on it.

Encrypted
Computers talk over mutual TLS 1.3; phones over TLS. Nobody on the network can read what you send.
Verified pairing
You compare a 6-digit code on both screens once. After that, every connection checks the other device's certificate fingerprint, not its name or IP.
Nothing outside the folder
Incoming file names are cleaned and the code is fuzz-tested. On Windows, received files get the Mark of the Web, like browser downloads.
Only you can use it
The interface is reachable only from your computer, needs a session secret, and refuses requests from other websites.
Hard to guess, quick to expire
A phone's QR key lasts 10 minutes, dies after 8 wrong guesses, and each phone is also approved on the computer.
No accounts, no tracking
There is no server and no telemetry. Everything Pasla knows stays in a folder on your computer.

Questions

Does Pasla need an internet connection?

No. Your devices only need to be on the same network, through the same router or switch, over cable or Wi-Fi. Pasla works on networks with no internet at all.

Is it really free?

Yes. Pasla is open source under the MIT licence. There are no accounts, ads, paid tiers or tracking.

Windows warns me about the file. Is it safe?

New programs that are not code-signed often get a SmartScreen warning at first, and some AI-based antivirus engines guess wrong about them. All the source is public: you can check the file against SHA256SUMS.txt or build it yourself.

Why does my phone say the connection is not private?

Your computer made its own certificate, so the phone's browser cannot check it against a public authority. The connection is still encrypted, and the 4-digit number you compare is what proves you reached the right computer.

What happens when I close the window?

Pasla quits a few seconds later; running transfers finish first. If other devices should be able to send you files while the window is closed, turn on Keep running after I close the window in Settings.

Is there a macOS version?

Not yet. Until there is, a Mac can join like a phone: scan or open the link that Connect a phone shows, in any browser.

My devices are on different subnets.

Routers do not pass on the broadcasts Pasla uses to find devices. Add the other device by its IP address once; after pairing, Pasla looks for it at its last known address.
